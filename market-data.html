Payroll Pricing: What Employers Actually Pay

A payroll bill has two parts that buyers routinely confuse: the subscription for running payroll, and the cost of the people on it. The subscription is the small number. Employer compensation — wages plus benefits — is the large one, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics measures it every quarter. This page keeps the two apart, and shows both.

Methodology

Included: BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation (the official quarterly measure of what employers pay per hour, wages plus benefits) and published vendor list prices for a named small-business payroll provider. Excluded: quote-only enterprise payroll and PEO pricing, because no comparable published price was verified this session; internal payroll-staff salaries; and tax rates, which vary by jurisdiction. Where a prior-period figure came from the same BLS release series it is shown; where only a current published price was verified, the row is marked Baseline rather than given an invented prior figure.

What the numbers mean

The gap that matters is regional. A Northeast employer paid $54.76 an hour in total compensation in June 2026 against $41.85 in the South — a $12.91 spread on the same hour of work. That spread, not the payroll software subscription, is what moves a hiring budget. The subscription, even at the top Gusto tier, is small beside it: $202 a month for a single employee on Premium, against roughly $8,000 a month in compensation for a full-time private-industry worker at the national average rate. Buyers who shop payroll on the subscription alone are pricing the envelope and ignoring the letter inside.
